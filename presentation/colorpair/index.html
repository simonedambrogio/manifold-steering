The color pair experiment

A reproduction and extension of Goodfire (2026) · Manifold Steering Reveals the Shared Geometry of Neural Network Representation and Behavior

A controlled test of the paper's central claim. A network given no geometric hint will, when the task demands it, fold its representations into the right shape, and that shape then governs its behavior. Scroll through the task, the model, and the geometry we recover from it.

1 · The task

Twenty-four colors live on a hidden ring. Each trial shows two pairs of colors, and the model must pick the pair whose colors are closer around the ring.

The ring is never given. Colors arrive as one-hot codes, so every color starts equally far from every other. The only place the ring exists is in which answer is rewarded.

2 · The seam

The giveaway is the seam. A pair like (color 23, color 1) is near around the ring but far on a line. A model that learned only a line gets these wrong. To get them right it has no choice but to fold the colors into a ring, which is exactly the folding geometry the paper is about.

3 · The model and how we train it

Deliberately free of any geometric prior. A shared encoder φ maps each one-hot color to a point in ℝ8, and a learned comparator g scores how close two embedded colors are. The choice is sigmoid(g(pair₁) − g(pair₀)), trained with cross entropy.

We withhold whole pairs (and add a k=1 control that cannot fold) so the model cannot memorize a lookup table. To generalize, it must place a reusable ring metric inside φ. Then we go look.

4 · The learned embeddings

Here is φ for all 24 colors, projected onto its top three principal components. Each diamond is one color's learned embedding. Drag to rotate.

5 · A ring appears

Connect the colors in ring order and they close into a loop. The seam color sits right next to color 0. The network folded the latent ring into its representations with no prompting. This is the activation manifold ℳh.

6 · The ring is in the behavior too

Using only the model's binary choices, never its internals, we reconstruct distances between colors and embed them with MDS. The same rainbow loop returns. The model's behavior, not just its representation, honors the ring. This is the behavior manifold ℳy.

7 · Steering along the manifold

Now the causal test. We move one color's activation along the fitted manifold from yellow to blue, and read the model's behavior from its choices only.

Press play: as the activation passes the midpoint color (green), P(action) for green rises to a peak, then fades. Steering on the manifold makes the model behave as each color it passes through.

8 · … versus a straight line

Now the linear steer appears below: a straight chord between the same two endpoints. It cuts through the interior of the ring, skipping the colors on the rim.

Press play: P(action) for green stays flat at zero. The linear path teleports from yellow to blue without ever behaving as green. Same endpoints, opposite behavior: only the manifold carries the concept.

9 · Every color in between

The same manifold steer, now reading five colors at once along the way: yellow, a yellow-green, green, cyan, and blue.

Press play: each color's P(action) rises and falls in turn as the activation sweeps past it, a clean ordered march, like one concept handing off to the next.

10 · The straight line skips them all

The linear steer appears below. Press play: only yellow (the start) and blue (the end) ever light up; the three colors in between stay flat at zero.

The chord jumps straight from one endpoint to the other through empty, off-manifold space, so the model never passes through the intermediate colors. The manifold visits every color; the straight line visits none.

Same conceptual ring, three views: built into the representation, expressed in behavior, and the axis along which steering stays natural. That triple is the paper's thesis, reproduced in a network we trained from scratch.
